# Asset intelligence and conversations

The asset page opens with four period returns, technical and fundamental conclusions, a historical price-risk panel, key facts, a compact official TradingView chart, news and an asset-specific conversation. **Ver gráfico** mounts the full chart workspace; it retains native/viewport fullscreen and a return to the summary. No custom price renderer or Cuantai datafeed is supplied to TradingView.

## Sources and calculations

`GET /api/data/asset-overview?id=…` collects the existing daily-history, fundamental and annual SEC adapters. Each source may be missing independently. No live failure falls back to synthetic data. The compact TradingView preview has its own data and may differ in source, timing, adjustment or coverage from the research cards.

- Price returns use completed daily closes only, conservatively excluding the entire current UTC date even after a venue closes. Daily compares the prior close, weekly seven calendar days, monthly one calendar month, annual twelve calendar months. Month ends clamp rather than overflow. The baseline is the last available close on/before the target, no more than seven days before it. Missing full coverage is `null`, never zero or a shorter-period annual result. These are price returns, excluding dividends, fees and FX. Split adjustment is whatever the provider explicitly reports. The raw quote is displayed separately and never mixed into split-adjusted history.
- Technical conclusions compare EMA 20 and EMA 50, with RSI 14 context, from at least fifty completed daily observations. They are deterministic historical readings, not model-generated orders. Values and as-of dates remain visible.
- Fundamental conclusions use the latest normalized annual SEC net income and operating cash flow, and annual revenue growth only when both values and comparable fiscal durations/year spacing exist. Both filings are cited. Unavailable cells remain absent. Statements for a CEDEAR describe its issuer in USD, not the certificate. Corporate P/E/profit methods do not apply to crypto or ETF holdings. No DCF or fair-value target is inferred.
- Historical price risk uses at most 253 equity or 366 crypto closes, at least sixty return observations, sample standard deviation of log returns annualized by √252 or √365. Excessive gaps suppress the metrics (over seven calendar days for equities; over one day for crypto). This is a gap heuristic, not an exchange-calendar completeness audit. Drawdown is the most negative closing-price/previous-running-peak variation in the same sample. Cuantai volatility bands are descriptive: below 20%, 20%–under 40%, 40% and above. They are not a complete risk rating. Solvency, spreads, liquidity, corporate actions, FX and investor suitability require other evidence.
- The synthetic demo has 280 daily observations and deliberately cannot supply a full annual return. Its data date is conspicuous. Real historical data is marked stale after two days for crypto or four for other instruments. Period dates remain visible even when stale. Financial statements retain the separate SEC freshness warnings.

For conceptual context see [Fidelity historical volatility](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/trading-investing/technical-analysis/technical-indicator-guide/historical-volatility). The formulas, windows and bands above define this implementation; they do not imply a vendor endorsement.

## Private conversations

Migration `0003_asset_chat.sql` adds `asset_chat_turns`. A turn stores a question and its verified structured answer atomically, scoped by authenticated owner, exact listing and demo/live data mode. `GET /api/data/asset-chat?id=…` returns the last thirty turns, oldest first, and indicates truncation. The model receives the last six exchanges and newly collected evidence. Anonymous/explicit demo UI conversations are temporary and use rules.

`POST /api/data/asset-chat` accepts only `{instrumentId, question, demo?, baseTurnId}`; the expected last turn is required, including `null` for the first. Owner identity and conversation history are derived on the server. Questions are trimmed and bounded to 1,000 characters; private writes use same-origin JSON, body-size limits and the existing daily AI budget shared with reports. A stale conversation version returns 409; final append is serialized under a database owner-row lock, with no lock held during model inference. The UI refreshes history and retains the unsent question on failure.

With `MARKET_DATA_MODE=live`, `OPENAI_API_KEY` and explicit `AI_MODEL`, the server uses one bounded OpenAI Responses structured-output request, `store:false`, a 25-second timeout and no automatic retries. History and retrieved headlines are treated as untrusted data. Source IDs and fact IDs are allowlisted; prose containing digits or generated URLs is rejected. Numeric cards are copied from deterministic evidence, and source links come from retrieved metadata. These checks reduce fabricated numerical claims; they do not prove semantic correctness of arbitrary prose. Invalid/model-failed output returns 502 and is not saved as a completed turn. Without credentials, or with demo data, the response is explicitly labeled **Reglas** and uses topic-specific summaries with a limited follow-up mechanism; it does not pretend to be AI.

News remains source-linked and dated, sorted newest first with future timestamps excluded. The compact panel shows at most five verified headlines. CEDEAR company news uses the underlying issuer; the certificate's own market events require an authorized local source. Missing Finnhub credentials or unsupported asset classes produce an explicit empty state, never fake headlines.

API implementation follows [OpenAI conversation state](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/conversation-state) and [structured outputs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/structured-outputs). Configured live model generation and licensed fundamental/news feeds have not been exercised without operator credentials. Unit tests mock model output to verify validation and bounded history; browser/API tests exercise real local storage, owner isolation, conversation conflicts, reload, the research overview and chart opening.
